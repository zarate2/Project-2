# Proposed portfolio mandate

This is an unnamed educational portfolio. Project 2 does not name a client; no real client or capital is represented. The app is a research and decision-support tool, not an order system.

| Policy | Current proposal | Basis and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Earn the 3-month Treasury rate plus 4 percentage points net of the added cost estimate | A 4-point premium is the base risk-compensation hurdle; +5 points is a stress case. It is a target, not a promise or forecast. The short Treasury rate is not locked for seven years. |
| Horizon | 7 years | Within the student's 6–7-year medium-term preference; equities and bonds can have longer horizons too. This horizon alone does not ensure a suitable outcome. |
| Withdrawals | None modeled | The assignment does not require a withdrawal schedule, so no payout reserve is included. |
| Eligible assets | U.S. Treasury bonds, investment-grade U.S. corporate bonds, domestic equities | Implemented with BIL (Treasury bills/cash proxy), IEF (Treasury notes), LQD (investment-grade corporate bonds), and VTI (U.S. equities). ETF proxies have fund and market risks. |
| Comparison | 60% VTI / 20% IEF / 20% LQD | A conventional 60/40 equity/fixed-income comparison. It is a benchmark for this exercise, not a claim that every institution uses this exact mix. |
| Risk limit | Annualized volatility no higher than 12% | A proposed guardrail. The student accepts a somewhat larger modeled risk budget to pursue the return objective, while keeping a firm ceiling. Risk above the ceiling would need a commensurate expected return; more risk does not guarantee more return. Volatility is not a maximum-loss limit. |
| Selection rule | Lowest estimated volatility among mixes that meet the net return hurdle and the risk limit | Avoid taking more modeled risk than necessary to meet the objective. This comparison is limited to the stated assets, estimates, and 5-point allocation grid; it does not prove that no lower-risk alternative exists outside that universe. |

Current defaults use a 60-month estimation window and 0.25% additional annual cost. Monthly adjusted prices through October 2026 are used for a proposed November 23, 2026 decision. Annual arithmetic estimates are screening inputs, not a seven-year compound forecast. We have not validated realized seven-year outcomes, a rolling strategy, liquidity, trading costs, turnover, or tax effects.

The benchmark weights and 4-point premium are student-proposed policy choices; the 5-point premium appears only as sensitivity. No automatic relaxation is allowed: when no allocation meets the hurdle and risk limit, the result is infeasible and the committee should revisit the mandate deliberately.

Decision requested: approve or revise this research mandate before any paper portfolio evaluation. The present app grants no authority to invest.
