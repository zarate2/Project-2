## 0:25–1:20 — Mandate

The proposal is a seven-year, long-only portfolio using BIL, IEF, LQD, and VTI, with no scheduled withdrawals. The comparison portfolio is 60% VTI, 20% Treasury notes, and 20% investment-grade corporate bonds. The target is the dated three-month Treasury rate plus four percentage points, net of a proposed 0.25% added annual cost. The November 19 Treasury observation is 4.34%, so the screening hurdle is 8.34%. The annualized-volatility ceiling is 12%.

These are proposed policy choices. The Treasury rate is short term and can change; it is not a seven-year guaranteed return. The estimated returns are historical arithmetic estimates, not a compound forecast.

## 1:20–2:15 — Comparisons and selected result

The equal-weight portfolio estimates 3.21% net, and the inverse-volatility portfolio estimates 3.20%, so both miss the 8.34% hurdle. The 60/40 comparison estimates 6.44% net and 11.98% volatility. It is inside the 12% volatility limit, but it also misses the return hurdle.

The constrained screen selects 35% BIL and 65% VTI. Its estimated net return is 8.61%, and estimated volatility is 10.28%. It passes the model’s return and risk rules on the tested five-percentage-point grid. That is a modeled result, not evidence that the portfolio will deliver those returns.

## 2:15–3:20 — Stress results

The stresses show that the result depends on assumptions. With a 36-month window, the model selects 75% BIL and 25% VTI, at 8.44% estimated net return and 3.21% volatility. A one-point reduction in the VTI estimate gives 25% BIL and 75% VTI, at 8.66% net and 11.84% volatility. A one-point cost increase gives 25% BIL and 75% VTI, at 8.41% net and 11.84% volatility. Tightening the equity cap to 25% produces no feasible allocation. These are sensitivities using the same historical data, not independent validation.
